# Video series: "From two X-rays to a 3D pelvis"

Date: 2026-11-11 · Audience: Adir and Noa · Status: approved arc (chat, 2026-11-11), test shot approved

## Intent

Custom teaching videos replace generic YouTube lectures in the weekly session. Adir and Noa have **no computer-vision background**. Both know ML basics; Adir has also done a full ML/DL course. The series takes them from "what is an image" to every technique the finished project uses, **always on our own data**: the pelvis CT we project, the X-rays we simulate, the meshes we extract. Each video ends with the hands-on task for that unit's notebook.

The project is treated as **complete**: the series covers everything the final project needs. Nothing is planned to be "made later once we know more".

## Goals / non-goals / anti-goals

- **Goals:** build real intuition (visual first, then the formula); accurate content; production quality clearly above the HyperFrames attempts (smooth 60 fps, cinematic 3D, clean typography); Hebrew narration with English technical terms on screen.
- **Non-goals:** medical/clinical teaching beyond what the method needs; a public YouTube channel; covering CV topics the project doesn't use (detection, tracking, optical flow…).
- **Anti-goals:** visuals that look impressive but teach nothing; formulas shown without a picture of what they mean; any claim that is wrong or oversimplified to the point of misleading; videos longer than the attention they deserve. Target length is **6–10 min** each.

## Format (decided in the test shot)

- 1920×1080, 60 fps, H.264 CRF 16, AAC audio.
- **Narration:** Hebrew, Microsoft neural TTS via `edge-tts` (`he-IL-AvriNeural`, rate −4%). The narration drives the timeline: scenes are laid out from the measured segment durations. Swapping in a better voice later only means re-running TTS.
- **On-screen captions** in Hebrew (RTL) with English terms; equations typeset with KaTeX.
- **Look:** dark blue-black stage, ivory bone, cyan X-ray light, warm accents for "the thing to notice". Bloom, a subtle vignette and light grain. Font: Rubik, bundled locally.
- **Every video opens** with the series title card and "you are here" on the pipeline map. It **ends** with a recap card plus a "your turn" card naming the notebook TODOs.

## Data for the videos

- **Source:** TotalSegmentator small subset, **CC BY 4.0**. Videos may be shared, with attribution in the end card. Subjects s1371, s1350 and s0812 were fetched individually from the remote zip (`data/scripts/remote_zip.py`). s1371 is the "hero pelvis".
- **DeepFluoro (CC BY-NC-ND)** appears only in video 13 (real fluoroscopy). Those assets are **never committed**, so that video renders locally only.
- Derived assets (meshes, DRRs, slices, small volumes) are generated by `videos/prep/*.py` into `videos/public/data/<video>/`. TotalSegmentator-derived assets are committed so CI can render them; DeepFluoro-derived assets are gitignored.

## Rendering on GitHub Actions

- The repo is public, so Actions minutes are free. Runners have no GPU, so WebGL is software-rendered (`--gl=swangle`).
- A workflow `render.yml` uses `workflow_dispatch` with an input listing the videos. The matrix covers **video × frame-chunk**, with chunks of about 1,200 frames (20 s) so each job stays well under the 6-hour limit. Each job renders `--frames=a-b` to a chunk file.
- A final job per video concatenates the chunks losslessly (ffmpeg concat of identically encoded segments) and uploads the MP4 as a workflow artifact, then attaches it to a GitHub Release `videos-<date>`.
- Local rendering remains available (the ANGLE backend on the AMD iGPU) for quick checks and for video 13.

## Architecture

```
videos/
  src/lib/        shared: Stage (canvas + postFX + lights), CameraPath, Captions, Equation, TitleCard,
                  PipelineMap, RecapCard, YourTurnCard, timeline-from-narration, asset loaders
  src/v00 … v14/  one folder per video: Video.tsx (scenes), script.json (narration segments + cues)
  prep/           python asset builders, tts.py
  public/data/    generated assets  ·  public/audio/<video>/ narration mp3 + durations.json
```

Each `script.json` lists segments `{id, text, caption}`. A segment's duration comes from its mp3, so scenes reference segment ids and never hard-coded seconds.

## The series (15 videos)

Notation: **[3D]** three.js scene, **[2D]** animated diagram/plot, **[EQ]** equation beat, **[DATA]** uses our CT/masks.

### 0 · The big picture (unit 00)
Goal: know what we're building and why it's hard.
- [3D][DATA] Hero pelvis. The question: two X-rays → 3D?
- [2D] The inverse problem: a projection loses depth; many 3D shapes give the same shadow (a cube and a sphere casting the same circle).
- [2D] Priors: we know what pelvises look like, and a network learns that from about 450 CTs.
- [2D] The pipeline map: CT → DRR (AP+LAT) → network → 64³ occupancy → mesh → metrics. It reappears in every video.
- [2D] The plan: 8 weeks, the units, how sessions work (video → notebook → explain-back).

### Part 1: images and geometry

### 1 · What is an image (unit 01)
- [2D] Zoom into an X-ray until the pixels show; a pixel is a number; the image is a 2D array; (row, col) vs (x, y) coordinates; grayscale vs RGB.
- [2D][EQ] Resolution and spacing: pixels are samples of a continuous world, with size in mm.
- [3D][DATA] A CT is a stack of slices, so it's a 3D array of voxels; the axial, coronal and sagittal planes, with a slice sweeping through our CT.
- [3D] Voxel spacing (anisotropic: 0.8 × 0.8 × 1.5 mm); orientation (LPS/RAS); the affine maps voxel index to mm.
- [2D] A mask is the same grid with 0/1 per voxel (labels: hip_left, sacrum, …).
- Your turn: load NIfTI with nibabel, print the shape and spacing, find the pelvis labels, show the three planes.

### 2 · Where the CT numbers come from (unit 01)
- [3D] One ray through tissue; intensity drops; [EQ] Beer–Lambert for a single material, then for many (the sum becomes an integral).
- [2D] μ per tissue; the Hounsfield scale is μ rescaled so water = 0 and air = −1000; [EQ] HU = 1000(μ−μ_w)/μ_w and its inverse (what our code uses).
- [2D][DATA] HU histogram of our CT with peaks for air, fat, soft tissue and bone; windowing.
- [2D] Why the display uses the line integral (log), not 1−e^−L: the saturation we hit in the test shot.
- Your turn: `hu_to_mu`, clipping, histogram.

### 3 · Cameras and projection (unit 01)
- [3D] The pinhole camera: rays through a point; similar triangles; [EQ] x = f·X/Z.
- [3D] Perspective vs orthographic (parallel): what changes (magnification with depth); our first DRR is orthographic.
- [EQ][2D] Homogeneous coordinates and the intrinsics K (f, principal point) and extrinsics [R|t]; P = K[R|t].
- [3D] An X-ray source is a pinhole, the detector is the image plane, and the body sits in between (not in front of the camera).
- Your turn: project 3D points with K[R|t]; compare parallel vs perspective.

### 4 · The DRR: simulating an X-ray (unit 01)
- [3D][DATA] Parallel-beam DRR: sum μ along an axis (rows sweeping, as in the test shot); AP and LAT.
- [3D] Perspective DRR: rays from a point source; sampling along each ray (Siddon / trilinear); what DiffDRR does; why "differentiable" matters (video 13).
- [2D][DATA] Our NumPy DRR next to DiffDRR next to a real fluoroscopy frame (the gap, foreshadowing video 13).
- [2D] Intensity mapping: line integral → display; detector size and pixel spacing.
- Your turn: `parallel_drr(volume, axis)`; then render AP+LAT with DiffDRR on Colab.

### Part 2: 3D shape

### 5 · Representing 3D shape (unit 02)
- [3D][DATA] Four ways to hold the same pelvis: voxel occupancy (64³ cubes), point cloud, triangle mesh, implicit/SDF (a field you threshold).
- [3D] Resolution trade-offs: 32³ vs 64³ vs 128³ of our pelvis (memory grows as n³).
- [3D] Marching cubes: a cube's 8 corners give 256 cases, which become triangles; a sweep through our mask building the mesh.
- [2D] Cropping and resampling to 64³ around the pelvis (what the network sees).
- Your turn: crop + resample to 64³, marching cubes, export the mesh, view it.

### 6 · Measuring a reconstruction (unit 02)
- [3D] Overlap: [EQ] Dice = 2|A∩B| / (|A|+|B|) and IoU; overlap of two pelvises coloured TP/FP/FN.
- [3D] Surface distance: Hausdorff (worst case) vs HD95 vs ASSD; Chamfer distance on point clouds; NSD at a tolerance.
- [2D] Why they disagree: a thin missing wing barely moves Dice but blows up HD95; a shifted blob is the reverse.
- [2D] A clinical angle computed from the mesh; why Dice ≠ clinical accuracy (the NeurIPS 2023 benchmark claim).
- [2D] Sanity tests: GT vs GT must score perfectly; empty vs GT must score worst.
- Your turn: `dice`, `hd95` (via distance transforms), Chamfer, unit tests.

### Part 3: without neural networks

### 7 · From two views back to 3D (unit 03)
- [3D] Back-projection: smear each X-ray back along its rays; with 2 views the smears intersect.
- [3D][DATA] Silhouettes and the visual hull: carve the voxels that fall inside both silhouettes; what the hull gets wrong (concavities, the pelvic inlet).
- [3D] Why more views help (CT = hundreds of views); triangulation intuition; the ambiguity left with 2.
- [2D] Results: hull Dice/HD95 on our test set as baseline #2.
- Your turn: `visual_hull(ap_mask, lat_mask)` + metrics.

### 8 · The memorisation floor (unit 03)
- [2D] Nearest neighbour: compare the input DRRs to every training DRR, return that patient's pelvis.
- [2D] Distance in image space (L2 on downsampled DRRs, or features); the cost.
- [2D] Tatarchenko et al. 2019: single-view reconstruction networks often perform like retrieval; why this baseline tells us how much the network really learned.
- Your turn: `nn_baseline` + metrics, the first results table.

### Part 4: neural networks for vision

### 9 · Convolution from scratch (unit 04)
- [2D][DATA] A 3×3 kernel sliding over our DRR: edges, blur; [EQ] the convolution sum; stride, padding, output size.
- [2D] From MLP to CNN: weight sharing and locality (parameter-count comparison).
- [2D] Channels and feature maps; stacking layers grows the receptive field; pooling / strided convs.
- [3D] 3D convolution: the kernel is a small cube sliding through a volume.
- Your turn: hand-rolled conv2d vs `nn.Conv2d`; output-shape exercises.

### 10 · Encoder-decoder and U-Net: from 2D to 3D (units 04–05)
- [2D] Segmentation as per-pixel classification; encoder (downsample, more channels) → bottleneck → decoder (upsample).
- [2D] Upsampling: nearest, transposed convolution (with checkerboard caveat).
- [2D] Skip connections: why U-Net keeps detail.
- [3D] Our architecture: 2D encoder per view → lift features to 3D → 3D decoder → 64³ occupancy; two ways to fuse AP+LAT (concatenate vs per-view lifting along its ray axis); tensor shapes at every stage.
- Your turn: build the network in PyTorch; check shapes with a dummy batch.

### 11 · Training it (unit 05)
- [2D] Output: logits → sigmoid → occupancy probability; [EQ] BCE; [EQ] soft Dice; why combine them (class imbalance: most voxels are empty).
- [2D] Data: split by patient (leakage animation); augmentation that keeps the geometry right.
- [2D] The training loop; overfit-one-batch first; validation Dice per epoch; reading loss curves (over/underfitting).
- [2D] Colab/Kaggle in practice: GPU, the 4-hour run rule, checkpoints to Drive, seeds.
- Your turn: training step, the overfit test, the first real run.

### Part 5: experiments and honesty

### 12 · A controlled experiment (unit 06)
- [2D] One view vs two: change one variable, keep capacity, data, steps and seeds fixed.
- [2D] Variance: 3 seeds per arm, mean ± std, why one run proves nothing.
- [2D] The second comparison (LAT angle 91–100°, or 100 vs 400 training volumes).
- [2D] How to read and present a results table honestly.
- Your turn: run both arms, fill the table.

### 13 · Simulated vs real (unit 07) — **local render only (DeepFluoro)**
- [2D][DATA] A real fluoroscopy frame next to our DRR of the same CT and pose: noise, scatter, contrast, field of view, magnification.
- [2D] Domain shift: why a network trained on DRRs drops on real images (IXGS: 29.45 → 23.22 dB).
- [3D] Re-projection check: render the predicted shape back with DiffDRR and compare silhouettes (differentiable rendering).
- [2D] Uncertainty with MC dropout: several passes, a per-voxel variance map, and whether it correlates with error.
- Your turn: evaluate on DeepFluoro, compute the gap.

### 14 · Putting it together (unit 08)
- [3D] Full pipeline fly-through on a test patient, from two X-rays to the predicted mesh vs GT.
- [2D] The results story: baselines vs network, 1 vs 2 views, sim vs real.
- [2D] The report: structure, the license section, personal contribution, the reproducible notebook.
- [2D] The demo for class.
- Recap of everything learned.
